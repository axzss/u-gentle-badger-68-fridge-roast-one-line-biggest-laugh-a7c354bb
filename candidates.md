# Fridge Roast — candidates

All lines written from scratch for this entry. Gate: 1–200 characters, one line, roast aimed at the smart fridge itself (app / firmware / camera / ice maker / its opinions about the milk).

## Candidates

1. **(124 chars, CHOSEN — revised)** My fridge updated its firmware and turned the ice maker into a subscription: first cube free, the rest after a 30-second ad.
2. **(119 chars)** My smart fridge updated its firmware at 3 a.m. and now the ice maker only dispenses cubes after I watch a 30-second ad.
3. **(109 chars)** The ice maker went on strike after the last update — one cube a day, which the app calls 'mindful hydration'.
4. **(120 chars)** The fridge's camera scanned my milk, decided it was 'past its prime', and locked the door until I apologised to the app.
5. **(115 chars)** My smart fridge updated its firmware, forgot how to be cold, and now texts me every hour to ask if I'm proud of it.
6. **(84 chars)** My fridge's app just notified me that my milk has filed a formal complaint about me.
7. **(132 chars)** The firmware update gave my fridge opinions: it now rates my midnight snacks out of ten, and the ice maker refuses to participate.
8. **(98 chars)** My fridge's camera recognised my 2 a.m. snack, updated its firmware out of shame, and renamed me 'the midnight incident'.

## Why chosen

Candidate 1 wins on the judge's criteria, in order:

- **On-subject:** the joke is aimed squarely at the fridge — its firmware update and its ice maker. No generic appliance gag.
- **Punchline lands:** the turn is the freemium reveal — "first cube free, the rest after a 30-second ad" — a surprise that only lands on the last words.
- **Specific to smart fridges:** firmware updates and ice makers are the two most machine-rooted features in the brief; the gag only works on a fridge that updates itself.
- **Tight wording:** 124 characters, every clause earns its place — setup (firmware update), turn (subscription), payoff (ad-gated cubes). The revision from "one cube free, the rest behind a 30-second ad" to "first cube free, the rest after a 30-second ad" sharpens it: "first cube free" is freemium language, and "after" is one word tighter than "behind".
- **Fresh angle:** it dodges the two obvious cracks the brief warns about (ordering groceries, watching you) and instead roasts the monetisation creep every smart device is guilty of — deadpan, no explanation of its own joke.

The runners-up lost on: #2 is the same gag told longer and flatter (the ad arrives as a plain fact, not a subscription reveal); #3 leans on the app's voice but the punch ("mindful hydration") is a wellness cliché the judge has heard; #4–#8 all spend words on setup the punchline doesn't repay, or aim at the milk/camera in ways that drift toward the "watching you" obvious crack.
